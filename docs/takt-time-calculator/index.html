Takt Time Calculator

The pace a line must run to meet customer demand, the number of stations or operators it needs, and whether your bottleneck can keep up.

Takt time
–

How it works

Available time = (shift − breaks) × shifts Takt time = available time ÷ demand Stations = work content ÷ takt (round up) Max output = available time ÷ bottleneck cycle

Takt vs cycle time

Takt time is set by the customer; cycle time is how fast your process actually runs. If any station's cycle time is longer than takt, that station is a bottleneck and the line cannot meet demand without overtime, an extra shift or rebalancing.

Takt time in one sentence

Takt time is the available production time divided by customer demand: the rhythm at which one finished unit must leave the line. If demand is 1,200 units and you have 860 minutes, a unit has to come off the line every 43 seconds.

It turns a sales forecast into a design target for the line. Every station's cycle time must be at or below takt, and the total work content divided by takt tells you the minimum number of stations or operators.

Worked example

Two 8-hour shifts with 50 minutes of breaks each give 860 minutes, or 51,600 seconds, a day. Demand is 1,200 units and each unit needs 210 seconds of work in total.

ItemValue
Takt time (51,600 ÷ 1,200)43.0 s
Stations needed (210 ÷ 43, rounded up)5
Line balance efficiency (210 ÷ (5 × 43))97.7%
Max output with a 45 s bottleneck1,146 / day

The bottleneck station is 2 seconds slower than takt, so the line falls 54 units a day short. Moving a few seconds of work to another station fixes it without overtime.

Using takt time well

  • Recalculate takt when demand changes; a fixed line speed that ignores demand builds inventory or misses orders.
  • Leave headroom. Running every station at exactly takt means any small stop causes a shortfall, so many lines plan for 85–95% of takt.
  • Takt time is a demand figure. It does not change when the machine gets faster; cycle time does.

Frequently asked questions

How do you calculate takt time?

Divide the net available production time by customer demand for the same period. Subtract breaks and planned stops from the shift time first.

What is the difference between takt time and cycle time?

Takt time is the pace demand requires. Cycle time is how long a station or process actually takes. Cycle time must be at or below takt.

How many operators do I need?

Total work content per unit divided by takt time, rounded up. Balance the work so no station exceeds takt.

What is line balance efficiency?

Total work content divided by the number of stations times takt. 100% means no station waits; 85–95% is a realistic target.
