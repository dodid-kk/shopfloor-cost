LTL Freight Class Calculator (2025 NMFC Density Scale)

Density-based freight class for a pallet or crate under the 13-tier NMFC scale in force since July 19, 2025, compared with the old 11-tier scale.

Freight class (2025 scale)

How it works

Cubic feet = L × W × H (inches) ÷ 1,728 Density = weight (lb) ÷ cubic feet Class = density band on the NMFC scale

Density only applies to some items

The density scale classifies freight that is rated by density in the NMFC. Some commodities have their own item class, and fragile or hard-to-stow freight can be rated differently. Confirm the NMFC item with your carrier before booking.

What changed in July 2025

Less-than-truckload carriers in the United States price freight by class: the lower the class, the cheaper the rate per hundredweight. On July 19, 2025 the National Motor Freight Classification moved many more commodities to density-based classes and expanded the density scale from 11 to 13 tiers. The old top band, 30 pounds per cubic foot and denser, was split into three: class 60, class 55 and class 50. Very dense freight can now rate lower than before, while light, bulky freight can rate much higher.

Worked example

A standard 48 × 40-inch pallet stacked to 48 inches high and weighing 500 pounds occupies 53.3 cubic feet, so its density is 9.38 pounds per cubic foot: class 100. Adding about 34 pounds would lift the density to 10 and drop the shipment to class 92.5. Measuring and weighing accurately matters, because carriers re-weigh and re-measure and bill the corrected class.

Common mistakes

  • Measuring the box, not the pallet. Use the outside dimensions of the whole handling unit, including the pallet and any overhang.
  • Forgetting the pallet weight. A wooden pallet adds 30–50 pounds.
  • Assuming density always applies. Some commodities have a fixed class in their NMFC item. Check the item before booking.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate freight density?

Multiply length, width and height in inches, divide by 1,728 to get cubic feet, then divide the weight in pounds by the cubic feet.

What freight class is 10 pounds per cubic foot?

Class 92.5 on the current scale, which covers 10 to under 12 pounds per cubic foot.

Why did my freight class change in 2025?

The NMFC moved many items to density-based rating and added classes 55 and 50 for very dense freight, so the same pallet can now rate differently.

Does freight class apply outside the US?

The NMFC is used for LTL freight within North America. Other regions usually price by weight or volumetric weight.
