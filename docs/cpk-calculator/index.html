Cpk & Process Capability Calculator

Cp, Cpk and the expected parts per million outside specification, from the process mean and standard deviation or from pasted measurements.

How it works

Cp = (USL − LSL) ÷ 6σ Cpu = (USL − mean) ÷ 3σ Cpl = (mean − LSL) ÷ 3σ Cpk = min(Cpu, Cpl) PPM = normal tail beyond each limit × 1,000,000

What the numbers mean

Cp shows how wide the process is compared with the tolerance; Cpk also accounts for how far the mean sits from the centre. A Cpk of 1.33 is the usual minimum for production parts and 1.67 for safety or new-part approval.

What Cp and Cpk tell you

Process capability compares the spread of a process with the tolerance a drawing allows. Cp asks whether the process could fit inside the tolerance if it were perfectly centred: tolerance width divided by six standard deviations. Cpk also checks where the process actually sits, taking the nearer of the two limits. A process can have a good Cp and a poor Cpk simply because it runs off centre, and that is usually the cheapest problem to fix.

Worked example

A shaft diameter has limits of 9.90 to 10.10 mm. The process runs at a mean of 10.02 mm with a standard deviation of 0.025 mm.

IndexValue
Cp (0.20 ÷ 0.15)1.33
Cpu ((10.10 − 10.02) ÷ 0.075)1.07
Cpl ((10.02 − 9.90) ÷ 0.075)1.60
Cpk1.07
Expected out of specabout 690 ppm

The spread is acceptable but the mean sits 0.02 mm high. Re-centring the process to 10.00 mm would raise Cpk to 1.33 without any new equipment.

Using the result correctly

  • Check stability first. Capability only predicts the future if the process is in statistical control. Plot a control chart before quoting Cpk.
  • Use enough data. At least 30 measurements, ideally 100 or more from several shifts, gives a reliable estimate.
  • Normal data only. The ppm estimate assumes a normal distribution. Skewed characteristics such as flatness or runout need a different method.

Frequently asked questions

What is a good Cpk value?

1.33 is the common minimum for production, which means about 63 ppm out of spec for a centred process. 1.67 is often required for safety-critical or newly approved parts.

What is the difference between Cpk and Ppk?

Cpk uses short-term, within-subgroup variation; Ppk uses the overall standard deviation of all data. With a single set of pasted values this calculator gives the overall figure, which is effectively Ppk.

Can Cpk be negative?

Yes. A negative Cpk means the process mean lies outside the specification limits.

How many measurements do I need?

Thirty is a practical minimum; 100 or more across shifts and material lots gives a much more reliable estimate.
